The four models, and why we moved from one to the next

Step 1 · v1 RSM

Response Surface Methodology

What it is: one fixed quadratic equation per material (linear, interaction and squared terms of concentration, time, pH, dose). Terms that are not significant are dropped.

Good at: explaining which factors matter (the ANOVA above) and giving a clean optimum. It fits the lab data well (R² 0.74 to 0.96).

Problem: on conditions it was not trained on it is poor (5-fold CV R² 0.13) and can predict impossible values such as negative qe. A single smooth curve cannot follow the real shape of the data.

So we tried: a neural network, which can learn curved, interacting behaviour without a fixed equation.

Step 2 · v1 ANN

Artificial Neural Network

What it is: a small network of connected nodes, 5 copies with different random starts, averaged.

Better because: it predicts unseen conditions far better (CV R² 0.859, hold-out 0.918 vs RSM 0.727).

Problem: accuracy was good, not yet great, and a single network is sensitive to its random start and to how it is trained.

So we tried: in v2, a Gaussian Process and ensembles, plus giving the model concentration and dose together as one ratio.

Step 3 · v2 ensemble

ANN + Gaussian Process

What it is: average of the ANN and a Gaussian Process (a model that predicts a value and how sure it is). Both also see the concentration-to-dose ratio.

Better because: CV R² rose to 0.874, and it handles the extreme low-dose, high-qe corner best.

Problem: only a small gain, and 12 models had been compared with a single train/test split, which can flatter a lucky model.

So we tried: in v3, a fairer test (repeated 5-fold CV over whole conditions) and better ANN training.

Step 4 · v3 final

ANN with Adam + early stopping

What it is: an ANN trained with the Adam optimiser that stops as soon as it stops improving on held-back data, so it does not memorise noise.

Best because: highest score under the fair test: CV R² 0.903, hold-out 0.937, leave-one-out 0.903, average error about 4 mg/g.

Known weakness: it smooths towards the middle, so it under-predicts the very highest-qe, low-dose results. There, compare with v2.

Why it is the default: best on average and confirmed on data it never saw. Every claim still needs lab confirmation.
